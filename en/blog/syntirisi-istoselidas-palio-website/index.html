Website Maintenance: What It Includes, Costs and What to Do About an Old Site

22 August 202610 min readCodehouse Team
The short answer: Website maintenance keeps a site secure, up to date, fast and useful. An old or neglected site can create technical problems, lose visibility on Google and undermine customers’ trust before they contact you.

Many business owners remember their website only when something stops working. A form will not submit, an image is broken, the site does not display properly on a phone, or the content looks out of date. By then, however, the website may have been losing visitors and opportunities every day without showing an obvious error.

Website maintenance does not just mean “having someone change a line of text.” It is the regular checking of technology, security, performance, content and the visitor journey. For a small business in Athens, Thessaloniki, Crete or anywhere else in Greece, that means the right information is available when customers need it.

This guide explains what website maintenance includes, the signs of an old website, when technical support is enough, and when an update or redesign is needed.

What is website maintenance?

Website maintenance is a set of preventive and corrective tasks that keep a site working. It includes technical checks, software updates, backups, security monitoring, bug fixes and content updates.

The exact package varies by platform. A simple custom site, a WordPress website, an e-shop and a site with online bookings all have different needs. A good website technical support service should explain exactly what is checked and how often.

Preventive and corrective support

  • Preventive maintenance: updates, backups, monitoring and checks before a problem appears.
  • Corrective support: fixing bugs, broken links, form problems or mobile display issues.
  • Content maintenance: changes to services, prices, hours, photos, news and contact details.
  • Improvement: changes to speed, UX, SEO and conversions based on data and business goals.

Why does a business need website maintenance?

A website is often where customers confirm that a business is active and trustworthy. If they find old opening hours, the wrong phone number, expired offers or broken links, their impression is affected—even if the business itself operates perfectly.

Regular support protects four aspects of your online presence:

  1. Security: reduces risk from outdated software, known vulnerabilities and data loss.
  2. Availability: helps detect errors, downtime and issues with forms or integrations quickly.
  3. Experience: keeps the site readable, responsive and easy to use on a phone.
  4. Business performance: keeps CTAs, services, products, bookings and contact details correct.

The cost of neglect is not always a repair bill. It can be a missed call, an abandoned booking or a visitor who does not return because they think the business is inactive.

9 signs your website is old

There is no absolute age at which every website becomes old. A site can be several years old and work well, while a newer site can have serious problems. These signs are more useful than the build date:

1. It does not work properly on mobile

Text that needs zooming, hard-to-tap buttons, horizontal scrolling or a form that is uncomfortable to complete show that the site is not truly responsive. For many businesses, mobile is the customer’s first point of contact.

2. The information is out of date

Old prices, incorrect hours, services no longer offered or an unchecked email address create immediate friction. Updating a website is part of maintenance, not a one-time task.

3. It is slow or performs poorly on mobile

Large images, unnecessary scripts, old hosting and poor caching can make a site slow. Read our website speed and Core Web Vitals guide for LCP, INP, CLS and practical fixes.

4. You cannot update content easily

If every change needs specialist intervention or you are afraid of breaking the page, the website no longer serves the business well. Easy updates matter for menus, services, portfolio, blog and contact information.

5. It has broken links or features that do not work

Check forms, phone numbers, email links, social profiles, PDFs, maps and booking buttons. One broken link can interrupt the visitor journey just before they get in touch.

6. It lacks basic technical SEO

Non-descriptive titles, duplicate pages, incorrect canonicals, an incomplete sitemap, poor heading hierarchy or indexing problems make organic visibility harder. Technical maintenance does not replace SEO strategy, but it prevents basic obstacles.

7. It uses old or vulnerable technology

Old CMS, plugin, theme or library versions can create compatibility and security problems. Updates should be made with a backup and checks—not blindly on a live site.

8. It no longer represents the business as it is today

If the team, services, service area, photos or portfolio have changed, the website no longer represents the business. Visitors compare what they see with recent information on Google and social media.

9. You do not measure what visitors do

Without analytics or basic conversion tracking, you do not know whether visitors find the phone number, complete the form or abandon at a particular point. Maintenance should be tied to useful measures, not just whether the homepage opens.

What does proper website maintenance include?

A practical website maintenance checklist separates recurring checks from changes made when needed.

Technical and security checks

  • Check availability and basic server errors.
  • Backups with a clear restoration process—not merely the assumption that “they exist.”
  • Update CMS, plugins, themes or dependencies where applicable.
  • Check SSL/HTTPS, file permissions and suspicious changes.
  • Test contact forms, email delivery, phone numbers and integrations.

Performance and mobile experience

  • Check images, lazy loading, caching and compression.
  • Check Core Web Vitals—not just one lab score.
  • Test on a real smartphone with a moderate connection.
  • Check that CTAs, forms, menus and bookings work with touch.

Content and SEO

  • Update services, prices, hours, photos and service areas.
  • Check title, meta description, H1, headings, canonical and internal links.
  • Check sitemap, robots.txt, broken links and pages that should not appear.
  • Keep business name, phone, address and Google Business Profile consistent.

How is maintenance connected to speed, SEO and customers?

Website technical support does not automatically promise better rankings. It does help prevent technical issues that undermine good content and SEO work.

An old website can affect organic visibility in several ways:

  • Slow loading and a poor mobile experience increase abandonment.
  • Broken links and incorrect redirects hinder navigation and crawling.
  • Old or duplicate content confuses a page’s topic and intent.
  • Inaccurate business information reduces trust and local discovery.
  • Broken features reduce calls, bookings and enquiries.

Think of maintenance as the foundation. Content answers the question, SEO helps the page get found, and good technical health lets visitors complete the action.

Maintenance or redesign? Which does your site need?

Not every old website needs a complete rebuild. The right decision depends on whether the problem is limited or structural.

Maintenance is enough when:

  • The underlying technology is supported and secure.
  • The site works properly on mobile and mainly needs updates.
  • Pages have a sound structure and need speed or content improvements.
  • Core forms, bookings and integrations can be maintained.

An update or redesign is needed when:

  • The site is not responsive and fixing it would cost more than a proper new foundation.
  • The platform or code is unsupported and presents serious security risks.
  • Navigation can no longer serve today’s services and audience.
  • The business has changed and needs a different structure, branding or functionality.
  • Speed, mobile experience and conversions cannot be meaningfully improved without changing the architecture.

A redesign should not mean losing your existing organic visibility. Before rebuilding, record important URLs, plan proper redirects, preserve useful content and check the sitemap. If a rebuild is the right choice, review our business website development process with a clear scope and technical foundation.

In practice, start by checking the site on mobile and desktop: test forms, phone numbers, key pages and any booking or payment flows. Then check the technology and backups before making changes. If the issue is one form, old copy or improvable speed, targeted maintenance may be enough. If there is no secure, supported foundation or the overall structure and experience need to change, compare piecemeal fixes with an organised redesign.

How much does website maintenance cost?

There is no single price for every business. Maintenance cost depends on the type of website, platform, number of changes, monitoring level and whether hosting, domain, backups and technical support are included. A small static site, a CMS with plugins and an e-shop have different risk profiles and needs.

For a fair comparison, distinguish regular recurring support from one-off work such as recovery after a fault, a platform upgrade or redesign. Ask for the frequency of checks, how backups are taken and restored, support hours, and whether the domain and hosting are in your name or included in the service. There is no reliable general price without reviewing the site and defining a clear scope.

Before agreeing, ask for clear answers to:

  1. Which technical updates are included?
  2. How many backups are kept, and for how long?
  3. Are content changes included or charged separately?
  4. Who responds to urgent problems, and how quickly?
  5. Are hosting, domain, SSL and monitoring included?
  6. Are speed, mobile functionality and basic SEO checked?

The cheapest option is not always the most economical if it excludes backups, security or a real response when something goes wrong. On the other hand, a large package with no clear scope may charge for services you do not need.

Maintenance and local SEO/GEO for Greek businesses

An active, up-to-date website gives search engines and AI systems more reliable information about a business: what it offers, where it operates, whom it serves and how a user can contact it.

For local searches such as “website technical support Athens,” “website maintenance Thessaloniki,” “web support Crete” or “business website redesign,” consistency matters. Check that your site, Google Business Profile and other business profiles do not show different phone numbers, hours, addresses or services.

For better understanding by search engines and AI/GEO answers, content should be specific and current. Use natural questions, clear sections, actual services and accurate service areas. Avoid artificial repetition of the same keyword or pages that differ only by city name.

Website maintenance FAQs

Does a website need maintenance?

Yes. A website needs regular checks for security, backups, updates, links, forms, speed, mobile functionality and accurate content. Frequency depends on its technology and features.

What does website maintenance include?

It can include software updates, backups, security checks, bug fixes, monitoring, content changes, speed checks, mobile testing and technical SEO.

When does a website need a redesign?

When it does not work properly on phones, cannot be updated, uses unsupported technology, has serious security issues or no longer serves the business’s goals. Not every old site needs a complete rebuild.

How much does website maintenance cost?

There is no single price: it depends on the platform, work required, support level and whether hosting, domain or backups are included. Separate regular recurring support from emergency repairs or redesign, and ask in writing what is covered and what costs extra.

What should I do if my website is old?

First check whether key pages, forms and contact details work on mobile, whether the technology is supported and whether backups exist. If the problems are limited, maintenance and targeted fixes may be enough. If the platform is unsupported or the structure no longer serves the business, assess a redesign with URL inventory and a redirects plan.

Can maintenance improve SEO?

It can fix technical issues with speed, mobile functionality, indexing, links and access to content. It cannot guarantee a particular ranking, because SEO also depends on content, search demand, competition and credibility.

Not sure whether you need maintenance or a new site?

A practical review of security, speed, mobile experience, SEO and content can show whether targeted improvements are enough or a redesign is needed.
