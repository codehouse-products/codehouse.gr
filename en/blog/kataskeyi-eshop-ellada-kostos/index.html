E-shop development is not simply creating an attractive page with products. It is creating a digital store where visitors can quickly find what they are looking for, trust your business, pay securely and receive their order correctly. For a small or medium-sized business in Greece, the right solution needs to be functional, cost-effective and flexible enough to grow with sales.

This guide explains what an e-shop includes, which features are worth paying for, what determines the cost of an e-shop and how Codehouse can handle design, development, online payments, basic SEO and technical support under clear terms.

This guide is for businesses comparing quotes and wanting to understand the overall requirements before committing. An e-shop does not only have a development cost: it needs day-to-day product and order management, agreed payment and shipping processes, and a plan for operating expenses after launch.

What a modern e-shop includes

A professional online store has two sides: the one customers see and the one you manage. The first needs to be fast, clear and easy to use on mobile. The second should let you update products, prices, stock and orders without needing a developer for every small change.

ElementWhat it does for the business
Homepage and categoriesPresent the brand and guide customers to the right product.

Payments and shippingConnect the store to cards, cash on delivery, courier or other options.

SEO, analytics and securityHelp customers find you, measure what works and operate more reliably.

The core features you need

1. A product catalog that is easy to navigate

Every product needs a clear title, description, photos, price, availability and a clear purchase button. If it has options such as size, color or a different pack, they should be presented without causing confusion. Categories and filters become especially important as the catalog grows.

2. A cart and checkout with little friction

Many e-shops lose sales at checkout because they ask for too much information or hide shipping costs until the final step. A good e-shop with online payments shows the total cost early, offers clear payment options and works properly on a small screen. Buying should be easy both for customers paying by card and for those choosing cash on delivery.

3. Shipping, courier and order updates

The experience does not end when “Complete order” is clicked. The e-shop needs understandable rules for shipping charges, free delivery, cash on delivery, store collection and returns. If you need an e-shop with courier service, the connection or shipping process should be agreed from the beginning, including areas, weights and potential costs.

4. Product and order management

Day-to-day management matters as much as design. You need a way to add products, change prices, update stock, create discount codes and check the status of each order. If you have many products or a physical shop, you may need a connection to an ERP, warehouse or invoicing system. These integrations are not essential for every first e-shop and are best added when there is a real need.

5. Security, policies and trust

SSL/HTTPS is essential, but it is not the only element of trust. Visitors should be able to find business details, contact methods, terms of use, privacy policy, shipping, returns and payment information. Codehouse can organize the presentation of these pages, but final legal wording and compliance should be checked by your own accountant or legal adviser where needed.

What determines the cost of an e-shop?

There is no responsible single answer to “how much does an e-shop cost?” without knowing the project. A store with 20 products and a simple shipping process differs from one with hundreds of products, complex variants, B2B pricing, multiple warehouses and ERP integration.

The main factors that affect a quote are:

  • Number and type of products: simple products, variants, bundles, digital downloads or services have different requirements.
  • Payments: card, bank transfer, cash on delivery, wallet or a combination.
  • Shipping: one flat rate, zones, weight, free-shipping threshold, collection or courier integration.
  • Content: whether photos and descriptions already exist or need to be created and edited.
  • Design and brand: ready-made foundation, tailored design or fully custom experience.
  • Integrations: ERP, warehouse, newsletter, analytics, marketplace feeds or marketing tools.
  • Support: who updates products, fixes problems and answers when you need help.

Ready-made package or custom development?

A ready-made platform or package based on a tested template may suit common needs: catalog, cart, basic payments and standard shipping. It may reduce initial implementation time, but check whether the price includes customization, licenses or subscriptions, add-ons and future changes.

Custom design or development is useful when a business has a specific purchasing flow, unusual product variants, complex pricing or essential integrations. It provides more control but requires more planning, development and testing. It is not automatically a better or cheaper option: the right criterion is whether it meets actual requirements without building features that will not be used.

Initial cost and recurring expenses

Ask for one-off work—such as design, development, initial content entry and testing—to be listed separately from recurring operating expenses. Depending on the solution, there may be domain renewals, hosting or platform subscriptions, payment-provider transaction fees and costs for add-ons, technical support or updates. Fees and terms vary by provider, so do not assume they are included without written confirmation.

What should be ready before implementation begins?

To organize the project properly, gather business and contact details, policies for review by your relevant adviser, product photos and information, prices and availability. You will also need to decide who handles the domain and hosting, which provider processes payments, how shipping is calculated and who manages returns and orders. Access to a bank, payment provider, courier or ERP is arranged by the business and the respective providers, according to the agreed implementation.

How long does it take to prepare?

Development time depends on how quickly the design, features and content are finalized, as well as the number of products, variants, approvals and tests for payments, shipping and integrations. A limited initial scope with ready, approved materials is usually easier to schedule than a store with complex workflows or ERP. An accurate estimate is provided after requirements are recorded; it is not responsible to promise one timeline for every e-shop.

When is it worth launching an e-shop?

A launch makes sense when there is a clear audience and product, a way to fulfil and ship orders, and time or a team to manage stock, content and customer service. Before investing in complex features, you can start with a viable set of products and processes, then expand when data and needs justify it. Building the store by itself does not guarantee sales.

The economical option is not always the lowest initial price. It is the solution that includes the features you need today, does not charge you for unused extras and lets you expand later. Also see the website cost guide to understand which charges to request in writing before starting.

An economical e-shop is not “cheap at any cost.” It is a well-defined project without unnecessary features, hidden fees or dependence on one provider for every change.

SEO and GEO so customers can find your products

An e-shop should not only work when someone already knows its URL. It should be understandable to Google, Bing and AI systems that answer users’ questions. This requires e-shop SEO across categories, products and technical infrastructure.

  • Keywords with purchase intent: “e-shop development Greece,” “[product] online,” “[product] Athens” or other phrases that genuinely fit your market.
  • Unique titles and descriptions: do not copy the same text across every product and category.
  • Structured data: Product, Offer, BreadcrumbList and Organization markup when it matches visible content.
  • Technical speed: small images, clean code, mobile-first layout and a fast path from product to checkout.
  • Content that answers questions: size guides, shipping times, FAQs, returns and comparisons help both customers and search engines.
  • Local and commercial signals: clear company information, service areas and connections to your online presence when the e-shop also has a physical store.

GEO does not mean filling the site with artificial repetition. It means clearly explaining what products you sell, whom they are for, where you serve and what visitors should do. The right structure and CTAs help users move from search to purchase. No responsible company can guarantee a specific Google position, but it can build sound foundations for organic visibility.

Checklist before requesting an e-shop quote

To receive a useful, comparable quote, prepare the following information:

  1. How many products will you have at the start, and how many do you expect within the first year?
  2. Do you have product photos, descriptions, prices, codes and stock information ready?
  3. Which payments do you want to accept and through which bank or provider?
  4. How will shipping be calculated and which courier serves you?
  5. Do you need a connection to ERP, warehouse, invoicing or a marketplace?
  6. Who will update products, stock and offers after launch?
  7. Which markets or areas do you want to serve: Greece, Cyprus or abroad?

With these answers, Codehouse can recommend the right architecture and separate essentials from optional features. If you do not have all the answers, that is fine: through the project questionnaire, we can start with the business need rather than an arbitrary platform.
