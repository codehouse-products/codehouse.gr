Free Website for a Business: Checklist Before You Choose

28 August 202612 min readCodehouse Team
The short answer: Yes, you can start with a free website. The key question is not whether you pay zero at the beginning, but whether the solution gives you the control, credibility, security and features your business needs.

The phrase “free website for a business” sounds simple, but it can describe very different things: a profile on a platform, a page on a subdomain, a trial that expires, a solution with ads, or a low-cost package called free because there is no initial fee.

That is why the right question is not only “how much does it cost?” It is: what exactly do I get, who has control, and can this page support the next stage of my business?

This checklist helps you make a considered decision. It does not claim that a free solution is always bad or that a professional website is always essential. It looks at when each may be enough and what to check before investing time or money.

What does “free website” really mean?

A free website usually means you do not initially pay for a basic creation or hosting plan. It does not necessarily mean every part of a professional online presence is free forever.

It may include:

  • a ready-made template instead of custom design;
  • an address under the platform’s domain instead of your own domain;
  • limited storage or bandwidth;
  • basic features without booking or sales-tool integrations;
  • platform branding or advertising;
  • limited support and less control over SEO, speed or data export.

There is another meaning too: software may be “free,” but still require time for copy, photos, settings, updates and troubleshooting. For a professional, that time is a real resource.

The 12 checks before choosing a free website

Before publishing a free website for your business, answer these questions. If you cannot find a clear answer in the platform or package terms, treat that point as a risk that needs clarification.

1. Domain: is it really yours?

A subdomain may be enough for a temporary portfolio or trial. For a business, your own domain is easier for customers to remember and gives you more control. Check who buys it, whose name it is registered in and whether you can transfer it to another provider.

2. Hosting: where is it hosted and what are the limits?

Every website needs hosting, even if you do not see it as a separate charge. Check limits on visits, storage, traffic, backups or uptime, and what happens when you exceed them. Hosting needs to be stable enough for the page to open when a customer needs it.

3. SSL and HTTPS: is the connection protected?

HTTPS encrypts the visitor’s connection to the page and is a basic requirement for forms and a secure experience. Check that SSL is included, renews correctly and does not trigger browser warnings.

4. Email: can you communicate professionally?

Email using your domain is not always included in a free solution. If communication happens only through personal email or a form whose messages you cannot access, there is an operational and organizational cost. Ask how leads are delivered and where they are stored.

5. Design and mobile-first experience

Check the page on a phone, not just on a computer. Text should be readable, buttons easy to tap, images should not break the layout, and the main CTA should be visible without needless searching. A template is useful only when it serves the content and brand.

6. Content and ownership

Can you update services, prices, hours, photos and contact details yourself? Can you download your text and images if you switch platforms? Content you create for your business should not be locked in without your understanding the terms.

7. SEO and indexability

A free website can appear on Google, but that does not happen automatically. Check whether you can set a unique title and meta description, use proper headings and canonical URLs, enable indexing, submit a sitemap, add alt text and connect Search Console. Without these, the page may exist but be hard to find. For broader strategy and the basics, see the guide to website SEO.

8. Speed and performance

Test key pages on a phone and a real connection. Heavy templates, scripts, pop-ups and unoptimized images can increase load times. Performance affects user experience, accessibility and the chance that a form will be completed.

9. Security and backups

Who updates the platform? Are backups available, and how long are they kept? Can you restore the site after a mistake or technical problem? If the page collects contact details, security and responsible data handling are not optional details.

10. Support and resolution time

“Support is available” is not enough of an answer. Check whether you can talk to a person, whether documentation is provided, when they respond and whether support is included or billed separately. If a form or domain stops working, resolving it quickly has business value.

11. Scalability and integrations

Consider what you might need later: a second language, more services, a portfolio, blog, appointments, payments, newsletter or analytics connection. You do not need to buy these now, but you should know whether the initial choice allows a smooth transition without starting from scratch.

12. Conversions and measurement

A website is not successful just because it exists. Can visitors call, submit an enquiry, book an appointment or buy? Can you see how many completed the action? Without a clear CTA and basic measurement, you will not know whether the website helps the business.

The real cost of a website

To compare solutions properly, divide the cost into four categories:

  1. Infrastructure: domain, hosting, SSL, email and any tools needed to keep the site online.
  2. Initial work: design, copy, photos, page structure, settings and content migration.
  3. Maintenance: updates, backups, security, form checks, changes and technical support.
  4. Opportunity cost: time spent building, fixing or searching for solutions yourself instead of serving customers.

Costs are not the same for every site, so ask for a written scope. Note what is included, what is optional, what is charged repeatedly and what happens if you cancel. For more detail, see the website cost guide.

Website, SEO, visibility, trust and conversions

A website does not automatically mean traffic or customers. The relationship works like a chain:

Website → Technical SEO → Content → Search visibility → UX → Trust → Conversion → Leads / Customers

If one link is missing, the result is limited. A beautiful website without content may not be found. A site with traffic but no clear CTA may not generate enquiries. A good page that loads slowly may lose visitors before they read the offer.

A free solution can be the right first step when you want to test a service or have a simple presence. But when the website is a core sales channel, you need control and a process that supports every link in the chain.

What should a modern professional website include?

Before considering a solution complete, use this checklist:

  • mobile-first layout that displays properly on different screens;
  • good performance and optimized images;
  • HTTPS and secure forms;
  • clear navigation and logical page hierarchy;
  • one prominent primary CTA;
  • contact form with clear confirmation;
  • SEO titles, descriptions, headings, canonical URLs and accurate alt text;
  • structured data where it genuinely matches the content;
  • analytics and a Search Console connection;
  • basic accessibility, including contrast, labels and keyboard use;
  • updates, access permissions and a security process;
  • backups and a maintenance plan.

Not everything needs to be in place on day one. But you should know which points your solution covers today and which may need to be added later.

Questions to ask before buying a website

If you are speaking with a professional or agency, ask for specific answers to these questions:

  • What is the page’s goal and which action will we measure?
  • What exactly is the scope: pages, copy, images and features?
  • Who owns the domain, hosting, accounts and content?
  • What is included in SEO, analytics and Search Console?
  • How will the website be updated after handover?
  • Who handles security, backups and technical problems?
  • How much do changes, maintenance and extra features cost?
  • Can you move the site and data if you change providers?
  • What is the timeline and what do you need to provide?

If the answer is only “we’ll see as we go,” ask for more clarity before committing. Transparency at the beginning reduces unpleasant surprises later.

When is a free solution worthwhile, and when is a professional one?

A free solution may be enough when:

  • you are testing a new service or market;
  • you need a temporary portfolio or landing page;
  • the website is not yet a core lead channel;
  • you accept limitations on domain, design and integrations;
  • you can handle the time needed for maintenance and updates.

A professional solution makes more sense when:

  • the site is the business’s main storefront or source of enquiries;
  • you need your own domain and consistent control of content;
  • you want to invest in SEO, content and local visibility;
  • you need bookings, payments, integrations or custom flows;
  • you lack time or technical experience for updates, backups and troubleshooting;
  • the current solution limits credibility or conversions.

The decision can also be gradual: start with the essentials, measure your needs and upgrade when you have a specific reason. The goal is not the most expensive website, but the right level of control and support for your business stage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I really create a completely free website for my business?

You can start with a free platform or profile, but the solution may have limits on domain, branding, storage, features and support. Check what is included before treating it as a complete professional presence.

Is a free website right for every business?

Not at every stage or for every need. It may be enough for a trial or temporary presence, but needs such as your own domain, SEO, bookings, sales, security and support may require a more complete solution.

What is the difference between a free and a professional solution?

It is not only the price. It concerns control of the domain and content, design, speed, SEO, security, backups, support, scalability and conversions.

Do I need my own domain and hosting?

Your own domain gives you more control and a more professional presence. Every website needs hosting, even if a platform or package includes it. Ask who owns the domain and what happens if you change providers.

Can a free website appear on Google?

Yes, but indexing does not guarantee a high ranking. It requires technical structure, useful content, good user experience, relevance and time. Some platforms limit SEO settings.

Does a free website need SEO?

At minimum, it needs basic technical and on-page optimization, such as a good title, headings, useful content, mobile usability, fast loading and a clear CTA. SEO does not guarantee traffic.

How much does a professional website cost?

There is no reliable price that fits every business. It depends on pages, content, design, features, SEO, maintenance and support. Ask for a written quote with a clear scope and recurring costs listed separately.

How often does a website need maintenance?

It depends on the technology and business changes. Regularly check backups, security, forms, links, analytics, content and performance rather than waiting for a problem.

When does a website need an update?

When it does not work well on mobile, is slow, is difficult to update, does not serve business goals or gets in the way of communication. Start a refresh with the problem, not just the age of the design.
