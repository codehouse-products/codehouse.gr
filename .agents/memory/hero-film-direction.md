---
name: Hero film direction
description: Art direction for CODEHOUSE's cinematic hero backgrounds.
---

Prefer tightly cropped, quiet tactile macro shots with one clear subject and little visual information. The graphite drawing film is the preferred reference; preserve it rather than replacing it with a new concept. Fingers and small fragments of a neutral face are appropriate companion subjects.

**Why:** The user explicitly preferred the drawing shot over busy keyboard geometry and wide meeting scenes, and requested closer, simpler compositions.

**How to apply:** When generating replacement hero scenes, match the drawing film's intimate photographic treatment. Keep imagery monochrome, with color restricted to cyan/violet neon effects or reflected neon light. Change scenes with direct cuts, not crossfades. Keep the existing annotated logo in the foreground.

The user subsequently requested stronger cyan/violet neon effects across every hero scene. Keep the monochrome base and restrained compositions, but do not treat the original subtle-neon preference as a brightness ceiling.

**Why:** After approving the cinematic direction, the user specifically asked for more neon in all videos.

**How to apply:** Use clearer neon rim light and soft optical glow rather than restoring busy geometric overlays. Close rear/over-the-shoulder software-work shots are allowed when requested; keep them cropped and focused on the creative action.

For typing, show natural varied touch-typing gestures, with the keyboard and keys outside the frame—not a repetitive two-button press. For eye closeups, the user wants the computer screen and recognizable programming code reflected in the eye.

**Why:** The user explicitly corrected mechanical-looking finger movements and requested a code-editor reflection, while confirming they liked the other quiet macro films.

Treat negative video prompts as directions, not guarantees of what is excluded from the frame.

**Why:** Asking for authentic typing repeatedly caused the generator to include keyboard hardware despite explicit exclusion instructions; asking for an eye reflection also introduced an unwanted screen-like outer frame.

**How to apply:** Inspect multiple moments of the generated footage before delivery. Use a verified finishing crop for strict object exclusions, while retaining enough hand movement or eye detail to communicate the intended action.
