Website for a Restaurant, Café or Bar: What You Need in 2026

Every day, thousands of people in Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos and other Greek cities open Google and search “good restaurant near me” or “café Glyfada open now.” If your business has no website, you are invisible in that search—regardless of how many years you have been open or how good your food is.

Why hospitality needs a website—and why Instagram is not enough

Instagram and Facebook are excellent tools for showing photos and keeping in touch with customers. But they do not appear in Google search results when someone is looking for a restaurant. Google Business (Google Maps) helps with the map, but cannot properly present your brand, show the full menu or take bookings automatically.

A website is the only digital “home” that belongs exclusively to you: it does not depend on social-media algorithms, lose reach or have its account closed. Most importantly, it can appear in Google’s organic results, exactly where customers search before deciding where to eat.

What a hospitality website should include

A website for a restaurant, café or bar is not just “a page with the address.” If you want it to turn visitors into customers, it needs specific elements:

1. An atmospheric hero section. The first image visitors see should “sell” the experience—not be a random background. Use a photo of your space, dishes or team, with a central message explaining what you are, where you are and why people should visit.

2. A digital menu. A complete menu with categories, descriptions and photos—or at least a link to a QR menu. Customers partly decide whether to visit by seeing what you serve. Read more in our article about QR menus.

3. A booking form or system. This is especially important for restaurants that take bookings. Every visitor who leaves without reserving a table is a potential customer lost.

4. Contact details and a map. Address, phone number and hours should be visible on mobile. More than 70% of traffic to hospitality websites comes from mobile devices.

5. Your story. Who you are, what cuisine you serve and the atmosphere of the space—a short text that gives the business a human face and supports SEO.

6. Reviews and trust. Include Google reviews or selected testimonials. A visitor who does not know your venue looks for confirmation from others.

Online bookings: the biggest opportunity

Imagine filling tables while you sleep. An online booking system that works 24/7—even at 2 a.m., when someone decides to reserve for tomorrow—sets you apart from competitors who respond only when they answer the phone.

Implementation can use a simple contact form (sufficient for smaller venues), a Google Calendar integration or a connection to a specialist booking system. For busy restaurants—especially in tourist destinations such as Mykonos, Santorini, Corfu and Rhodes—a booking system can literally double seasonal turnover.

Local SEO for hospitality: how customers find you on Google

Local SEO is the practice of appearing when someone searches with a geographic context—“Kolonaki restaurant,” “Nea Smyrni café,” “Thessaloniki waterfront taverna.” Without proper optimization, even a beautiful site may remain invisible.

How local SEO for hospitality works:

Local keywords on the site. Website copy should naturally mention the neighborhood, city and type of hospitality—for example, “Italian restaurant Glyfada,” “specialty coffee Exarchia” or “seaside-view restaurant Voula.”

Location schema markup. Code that tells Google exactly where you are, your opening hours and cuisine can increase the chance of appearing in the Local Pack (the three map results).

Connection to Google Business Profile. The website and Google Business Profile should have the same details (NAP: Name, Address, Phone). Inconsistency here can seriously harm rankings.

Fast mobile loading. Google prioritizes sites that load quickly on phones—and most hospitality customers search on their phones.

For steps specific to Google Business Profile—such as category, hours, menu, booking links and reviews—see the practical restaurant SEO on Google Maps guide.

A strong local presence helps customers find accurate information and an easy way to contact you or book. Its effect on calls and reservations varies by business and should be measured, not assumed.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a website if I have Google Business and Instagram?

Yes. Google Business puts you on the map but cannot present your brand, show your menu in depth or take bookings. Instagram does not appear in Google searches. A website is the only digital “home” you fully control.

How many days does it take to build?

With an organized collaboration, 5–14 days. Having photos of your space and dishes ready is key—it speeds up the process more than anything else.

Do I need professional photography?

Ideally, yes. Professional photos can significantly improve conversion rates—customers who see appetizing photos book much more often. If you do not have a photography budget now, we can start with good phone photos.

What if I want to change the menu?

Your website should give you an easy way to change prices, add dishes or update hours without calling the developer. This is a basic requirement we confirm on every project.

Do I need a website if my business is in a tourist area?

Absolutely—and even more so. Tourists from Europe, the US and elsewhere search Google for restaurants before they arrive. An English website (and ideally additional languages) is a strong competitive advantage in Mykonos, Santorini, Crete, Rhodes, Corfu and every tourist area.
