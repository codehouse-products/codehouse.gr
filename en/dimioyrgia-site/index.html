What a website needs today

A modern website should be useful before it is beautiful

Design attracts attention, but usefulness is what keeps visitors. A good website quickly answers the essentials: what the business is, what it offers, where it serves customers, how easily someone can ask for information and what happens after they get in touch.

That means a clear first screen, logical hierarchy, readable copy and calls to action that do not compete. It also means avoiding heavy elements that slow down mobile devices or burying information behind complicated animations.

Domain, hosting and security

Your domain is your business’s address, hosting is the space where the website lives, and SSL protects the connection. These are not last-minute details. Proper configuration, backups, updates and uptime monitoring reduce the risk of a website going offline or relying on an outdated technical foundation.

SEO and GEO from the start

Optimisation is not a sticker added after development. It starts with page naming and whether the content speaks the language customers use. It continues with technical details, internal linking and useful answers. For GEO, clear direct statements, specific services and information that search systems can understand and cite accurately are helpful.

A sound foundation does not guarantee a top ranking or a steady number of leads by itself. It does give a business a reliable base for organic search, local visibility, social campaigns and future marketing. For more on the importance of SEO for a business, see the related guide.

Pre-launch checklist

  • A clear H1 and understandable value proposition
  • Responsive layout and easy-to-use navigation
  • A CTA for the visitor’s primary action
  • Correct title, description and canonical
  • Optimised images and alt text
  • HTTPS, backups and essential security
  • Test the form, phone and email
  • Analytics and Search Console where appropriate
  • Check 404s, redirects and sitemap

After launch

A website is the foundation, not the whole strategy

A new website can make your business clearer and easier to contact, but it does not automatically generate traffic. To create more opportunities, it needs to work alongside your other efforts: Google Business Profile, social media, email, advertising, content, local SEO and helpful responses to people already interested.

After launch, track what visitors view, where they come from and where they drop off. Those insights can improve a CTA, service, form or page that does not explain your value clearly enough.

Frequently asked questions

Answers before you begin

Common questions about website creation, cost, timing and what a business needs after launch.

What does professional website development include?

It typically includes goal analysis, content architecture, design, responsive development, basic SEO, domain and hosting setup, SSL, testing and post-launch support. The exact scope depends on the business’s needs.

How much does website creation cost?

Cost depends on the number of pages, content, functionality and degree of custom design. A simple company website differs in cost from an e-commerce shop or complex platform, so a specific brief is needed before a proposal can be prepared.

How long does it take to build a website?

A typical company website can be completed in a few business days when materials and decisions are ready. Larger websites, e-commerce shops and projects with custom functionality take longer.

Will the website work properly on mobile?

Yes. A responsive, mobile-first approach adapts the structure, navigation, buttons, images and copy to phones, tablets and desktops.

Who handles the domain, hosting and SSL?

These can be handled as part of the project so that the domain, hosting and SSL are configured correctly and remain secure. Exact terms are agreed before development begins.

Will a new website automatically bring in customers?

Not automatically. A website is the foundation of an online presence, but traffic and conversions require good content, SEO, local visibility, a credible offer and ongoing improvement.

Can a website be built without finished copy or a logo?

Yes. We can start with the materials available and organise or create essential copy and visual direction, so the project does not stall because assets are missing.

What happens after the website launches?

After launch, we check functionality, security, key measurements and technical details. Ongoing support can then cover content updates, maintenance, SEO improvements and new requirements.

Can Codehouse work with a small business in Greece?

Yes. The process can be tailored to small businesses, freelancers, shops, hospitality venues, tourism services and companies that need a clear and professional online presence.
