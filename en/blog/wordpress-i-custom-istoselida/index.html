WordPress or a Custom Website? What Makes Sense for a Small Business

31 July 20267 min readCodehouse Team

Ask five website developers “WordPress or custom?” and you will get five different answers—and, strangely enough, each answer will match what that person sells. This article offers an honest comparison: where each option excels, where it falls short and what really makes sense for a small Greek business in 2026.

What each one means, in simple terms

WordPress is a ready-made content management system: install a theme (template), add plugins for the features you need, and you have a website. It is used by around 40% of the web—from blogs to large portals.

A custom website is written from scratch in code (HTML, CSS, JavaScript), built for your exact needs—with no ready-made templates, plugins or unnecessary features.

The comparison, point by point

CriterionWordPressCustom
Loading speedModerate—loads many files from plugins and themesExcellent—only the code it needs
Initial costLower (with a ready-made theme)Similar or slightly higher
Annual maintenanceNecessary—plugin/theme updates; otherwise, riskAlmost none—there is little to “break”
SecurityFrequent target for hackers due to popularity and pluginsSmaller attack surface
Editing content yourselfEasy via the admin panelRequires the developer (or a simple content management system)
Unique designLimited by the themeComplete freedom
SEOGood with the right setup (but heavy = slow = a penalty)Excellent—speed and clean code benefit Google

Where WordPress wins

WordPress is a logical choice when you need frequent, independent content management: if you publish several articles every week, run an e-shop with hundreds of products that you update daily, or need a very specific feature that already exists as a proven plugin. Its admin panel is user-friendly and lets you do a lot without touching code.

Where custom wins

For a typical small business—a café, restaurant, garage, medical practice or hair salon—that needs a fast, attractive brochure website for its services, menu or projects, a custom solution is better in almost every way. It loads extremely quickly (important for SEO and mobile visitors), needs no monthly maintenance or security updates, is not exposed to plugin vulnerabilities and looks unique—not like thousands of sites using the same theme.

The argument “but I can edit the content myself in WordPress” matters less than you might think: in practice, most small-business owners change something on their website only a few times a year—and a good partner can make these changes in a few hours, often at no charge.

Our rule: if your site is a shop window, go custom. If it is a publishing engine with daily content, consider WordPress.

And the cost?

The common belief that “custom is extremely expensive” belongs to the past. With modern tools and AI technology, a custom business website can now cost as much as—or less than—a well-built WordPress site with a premium theme and plugins, without annual licence renewal and maintenance costs. See real figures in our 2026 website cost guide.

Conclusion

There is no “right” technology—there is the right technology for your situation. For most small businesses that want an impressive, fast-loading presence without maintenance worries, custom is the cleaner choice. For businesses with substantial, constantly changing content, WordPress remains a powerful tool.
