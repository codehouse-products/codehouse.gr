Free Website: How to Get Started with Wix, WordPress.com or Google Sites

31 July 20266 min readCodehouse Team

You can get started without an upfront charge on a free plan—but check its limitations first.

If you are looking for how to make a free website, you can try the free plans from Wix and WordPress.com or use Google Sites. They do not offer the same tools, limits or publishing capabilities. Before choosing, consider whether you need a simple information page, a blog or a business presence with specific functions.

To get started yourself: (1) choose a platform based on the type of site, (2) organize its main sections and prepare text and images, (3) build a draft page, (4) check how it looks on mobile, and (5) confirm in the provider’s current terms which features—such as your own domain—require an upgrade. If the site is for a business, use the business website selection checklist before deciding.

What do you actually get for “free”?

Features vary by provider and may change. Use the table below as a checklist, not as a comparison with one answer that applies to every platform:

What you needWhat to check in the plan
Your own domain (mycafe.gr)Whether you can connect your own domain or need a paid plan; domain registration may be a separate cost.
Appearance and advertisingWhether provider branding or ads appear, and which design options are available.
Search visibilityWhether the published page can be indexed and whether you can set a title, description and basic content. Rankings do not depend on the plan alone.
Speed and mobileTest the actual published page on a phone; there is no single load time that applies to all free plans.
Email and supportWhether business email is included, which support channels are available and whether they cost extra.

The cost you don’t see: your time

Even without an upfront charge, you will spend time learning the editor, preparing text and images, organizing pages and checking how they work on mobile. The time required depends on your experience and the content you already have; do not rely on one general estimate of hours or cost for every project.

A free plan may be suitable for a simple start. But account for the time needed to set it up and keep it updated.

Then comes “upgrade now”

If you later need your own domain, more features or removal of provider branding, compare the current plans and terms before upgrading. Also check whether and in what format your text and images can be exported, what happens if you cancel the subscription, and who controls the domain. These options vary by platform and do not mean your content is automatically lost.

When is free actually worthwhile?

To be fair: if you are making a personal hobby blog, a page for a school event, or simply want to experiment—yes, start for free; that is perfectly reasonable. That is exactly what a free plan is designed for.

But if you have a business—a café, restaurant, garage or shop—check whether the solution meets your communication needs, supports your own domain, is accessible and can grow with you. A free plan may be enough for a simple temporary page, but not necessarily for the functionality you need in the long term.

The conclusion (which you probably knew already)

You can publish a basic page without an upfront charge, but whether it is suitable depends on your needs. Compare your time, the free plan’s capabilities, the cost of a potential upgrade and what a professionally built site would include. For the latter, see the website cost guide.
