QR Menu Costs in 2026: Platforms & What to Choose

6 August 20268 min readCodehouse Team

In 2026, a QR menu is no longer new—it is standard. But while the technology has gone mainstream, the market is full of options with different quality and costs that can easily confuse you. This guide gives clear answers: what it costs, what difference the choice makes, and what to ask for to get the best result—depending on where you are and what kind of business you run.

What is a QR menu in 2026—and what has changed?

The basic idea is familiar: a customer scans a QR code and opens the menu on their phone. But good QR menus in 2026 have moved well beyond that:

Photos of every dish—studies show a 30–40% increase in orders of dishes accompanied by a photo.

Filters and categories—vegetarian, allergens, vegan, chef’s recommendations: customers find what they want faster and order with greater confidence.

Multiple languages in one tap—essential in tourist areas.

Wi-Fi and social media links—customers scan and connect to Wi-Fi or follow your Instagram.

The first “QR menus” were simple PDFs. In 2026, that is not enough: a PDF is not responsive, has no photos, loads slowly and is hard to update.

Subscription platform vs custom QR menu: what should you choose?

The right choice depends on your situation:

Subscription platform (e.g. Dotmenu, Mr Yum, Menufy)—suitable if:

You want something ready in hours, brand identity is not a concern and you know you will use it for only a few months (for example, a seasonal venue). Keep in mind, however, that the menu is hosted on a third-party domain (e.g. dotmenu.gr/your-venue), does not help your SEO at all, and if you stop paying, you lose everything.

Custom QR menu on your own domain—suitable if:

You want a long-term solution, the menu on your own URL (e.g. your-venue.gr/menu), custom design in your colours, and SEO benefits. Codehouse’s service falls into this category: a custom digital menu owned by your business, with a one-off build and low annual hosting cost.

A QR menu on a third-party platform is like renting a sign for your storefront. As soon as you stop paying, it disappears.

Athens, Thessaloniki and the islands: what changes by region?

A QR menu needs a different approach depending on where you operate:

Athens & Thessaloniki (urban customers): Loading speed and mobile UX are critical—customers have high expectations. Allergen filters and vegan options matter. In busy areas such as Kolonaki, Glyfada, Kifissia and Pangrati (Athens), or Ladadika and Ano Poli (Thessaloniki), premium UX helps you stand out.

Mykonos, Santorini, Rhodes, Crete and Corfu (tourist customers): Multiple languages are a requirement, not an option. English at minimum, ideally with German or French as well. Tourists from Northern Europe and the US avoid venues without an English menu. The menu must also load quickly with a weak signal—a common issue on islands.

Seaside resorts & beach bars: Simplicity wins—few categories, large photos and quick decisions. Customers at the beach do not want to search for long.

Mountain or rural venues: Mobile internet may be weak, so make sure the QR menu works with a slow connection using caching and lightweight images.

What to ask your QR-menu developer

Before spending anything, make sure you know the answers to these questions:

Which domain/server will host it? It should be yours or transferable to your own hosting. If it is locked to a third party, you do not control it.

How do I change prices or add an item? Ask for a demo—it should be easy, in 2–3 steps, with no technical knowledge required.

What if I want to end the relationship? Do you get your code and data, or lose everything?

Does it support multiple languages? If you operate in a tourist area, this is not an extra—it is a core feature.

How does it look on older Android phones? Tourists do not always have the latest iPhone—the menu should work everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a QR menu if I have a small café or bar?

Yes. Even for small venues, a QR menu saves time, reduces printing costs and gives a professional impression. For bars and cafés with frequent changes (seasonal cocktails, daily specials), updating the menu in minutes without printing is a major advantage.

Can the QR menu show allergens too?

Yes—and in many EU countries, listing allergens is now mandatory. A well-designed QR menu includes this information in a readable format that is easy to update.

Can I have a QR menu without a website?

You can, but it is not recommended. Ideally, the QR menu should be on your business domain (for example, your-venue.gr/menu)—which also helps your SEO. If you do not have a website yet, this can be a starting point.
