How Much Does a Website Cost in 2026? A Complete Pricing Guide

31 July 20268 min readCodehouse Team
ContentsPrices at a glanceWhat affects the costThe hidden fees no one tells you aboutAnnual maintenance costsHow not to overpay

“How much does a website cost?” is probably the first question every business owner asks when deciding to go online—and the answer they usually get is the frustrating “it depends.” In this guide, we give you real figures for the Greek market in 2026, explain what affects the cost, and point out hidden fees to watch for before you sign anything.

Prices at a glance

In the Greek market, website development prices vary widely depending on the project and who builds it:

Website typeIndicative costDelivery time
Simple presentation page (one-page)€250–€8003–10 days
Business website (5–10 pages)€600–€2,5001–4 weeks
QR digital menu for hospitality€200–€6002–7 days
E-shop (online store)€1,200–€6,000+3–8 weeks
Custom web application€3,000–€20,000+1–6 months

Why such a wide range? “Website” can mean anything from a hastily assembled €200 template to a fully custom site with unique design, copy, photography and SEO. The difference shows in the result: how quickly it loads, how professional it looks and—most importantly—whether it brings in customers.

What affects the cost

Five factors determine around 90% of the final price. First, design: a ready-made template costs less but looks like thousands of other sites; custom design costs more but builds your brand. Second, content: if you need copy, photography and a logo, someone has to create them—with modern AI tools, this can now be done faster and more affordably than ever. Third, functionality: forms, bookings, payments and multiple languages each add work. Fourth, SEO: a site without basic optimisation is like a shop without a sign. Fifth, the provider: a large agency with city-centre offices has overheads; a student may charge less but often disappears after delivery.

The hidden fees no one tells you about

This is where many business owners end up paying more without realising it. Check the following before agreeing:

Who owns the domain and hosting? If the domain is registered in the developer’s name instead of yours, you are dependent on them. If you change providers, they may “hold on to” your site. Always ask for the domain to be yours.

What does “maintenance” include? Many companies charge €20–€50 per month for “maintenance” that in practice includes little beyond hosting, which costs €5. Ask specifically: how many changes per month are included? What is the response time?

Fees for every change. “I want to change a menu price”—€30. “Add a photo”—€20. If your site needs frequent changes (for example, in hospitality), make sure you can either make them yourself easily or have a plan that covers them.

Annual maintenance costs

Beyond the initial build, every site has recurring annual expenses you should know about from the start:

ExpenseAnnual cost
Domain (.gr or .com)€10–€25
Hosting€40–€150
SSL certificate€0 (Let’s Encrypt)–€100
Business email€0–€70
Maintenance / updates€0–€600

In total, a healthy site costs around €50–€300 per year in recurring fees. If you are quoted multiples of that, ask exactly what you are paying for.

How not to overpay

The best protection is asking the right questions. Before you agree to work together, request a written quote that clearly states what is included (design, copy, SEO, mobile responsiveness), who owns the domain, what maintenance and future changes cost, and when the site will be delivered. Compare two or three quotes—not just on price, but on what you get. To see what professional business website development includes, review the scope carefully before comparing prices.

The cheapest quote is rarely the most economical. A site that brings in no customers is expensive, no matter how little it cost.
